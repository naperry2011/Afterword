Nobody quit reading because books got worse

They quit because finishing one felt like nothing. Last page, close it, onto a pile. Three months go by before another one gets opened.

Afterword puts a small, satisfying thing at the end of the book that's worth walking toward.

Finishing counts

Two minutes of gentle questions and the book is properly closed. That tiny ceremony is what gets people starting another one the same night.

The shelf fills up

Every finished book adds a spine to a row you can see. It works better than a streak because you built it out of real books, not consecutive days.

Next is already picked

Cards from friends land in the empty slots at the end of your shelf, reasons attached. You never sit there wondering what to read.

Three questions when you close it

They appear the moment you mark a book finished. Answer one or all three. No blank page, no essay, and skipping is a completely normal thing to do.

FIRST

What stayed with you?

Not a summary. The image or line still sitting there a day later.

SECOND

Who should read this, and why them?

Naming one person turns a review into a recommendation. This answer becomes the card.

THIRD · OPTIONAL

What did it change?

Often the honest answer is nothing, and that's worth writing down too.

Put one down halfway? Same questions, and it still goes on the shelf. Quitting a book you aren't enjoying is how people keep reading, not how they stop.

The best book you read this year came from a friend

Not from a ranked list. Not from a 4.1 average. From one person who knows you, saying this one's yours, and saying why.

Sending one takes four seconds

Your answer to the second question is already written, so the card already exists. Pick it, send it, done. Someone gets a real reason instead of a link. It runs both directions too, because the cards people send you are what fill those empty slots at the end of your shelf.

Yours and nobody else's

You can be honest about a book when there's no audience watching you be honest. Your shelf and everything you wrote stay on your phone.
